                          FIDONET -- AN OVERVIEW
                          ======================

FidoNet is the way BBSs talk to each other. It exists because phone
calls cost money, and a long-distance call from Cleveland to a
single-line BBS in Sydney to read message bases is unsustainable.

FidoNet solves this with a relay tree.

[--[ ADDRESSING ]--]

A FidoNet address is "zone:net/node" or "zone:net/node.point":

    1:202/420       Tom Jennings, the original
    1:13/13         Some node
    2:201/100.1     A point off node 100 in net 201, zone 2

Zones partition the world: 1=North America, 2=Europe, 3=Pacific,
4=Latin America, 5=Africa, 6=Asia. Inside a zone, nets are regions;
inside a net, nodes are individual BBSs. Points are users who
receive mail through a node but do not themselves accept calls.

[--[ HOW MAIL FLOWS ]--]

When you post in an Echomail conference, your message is packaged
overnight, your BBS dials its hub, and the hub aggregates messages
from every node beneath it. Hubs forward upward. Up at the zone
gates, regional traffic gets exchanged with other zones via more
expensive routes. The next morning, the same packet flows back down
the tree to every node that subscribes to that conference.

A round trip from "post" to "every BBS in your conference can read
it" is typically 24 to 72 hours. Patience is a feature.

[--[ ZONE MAIL HOUR ]--]

Every FidoNet node is required to be available for mail exchange
during a fixed daily hour (Zone Mail Hour, ZMH -- 0900 UTC zone-1).
During ZMH, the line is closed to human users; only mailers may
connect. This is what makes the routing work at all: you can count
on the node above you being there.

[--[ NODELIST ]--]

The Nodelist is a giant file -- updated weekly -- that maps every
addressed node to its phone number, name, sysop, baud rate,
connection capabilities, and operating hours. You compile it on
your machine into a binary index that your mailer reads. Without
the Nodelist, no FidoNet.

[--[ POLICY ]--]

FidoNet has a written Policy document (POLICY4 as of 1989) that
covers behavior, dispute resolution, and how to remove a node from
the net. People take it seriously, the way one takes seriously the
rules of any organization where the alternative to following them
is being on no organizational chart at all.

[--[ WHY IT MATTERS ]--]

FidoNet is what most BBS users call "the net" without irony. The
Internet is a thing university people have. FidoNet is what you
have. There is overlap (UUCP gateways exist, and you can email
"user@host.fidonet.org" from a Unix box if you know somebody who
runs one of those gateways), but in 1991 the two communities barely
acknowledge each other.

This will not stay true much longer.

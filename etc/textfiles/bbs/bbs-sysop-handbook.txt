                       BBS SYSOP HANDBOOK -- EXCERPT
                       =============================

A Bulletin Board System is a single computer running on a single
phone line. Users dial in one at a time. There is one of you and
many of them. The handbook below is what every sysop figures out in
their first six months. Distilled to save you the time.

[--[ HARDWARE ]--]

A 286 is enough for a single-line board. A 386 is comfortable. You
will want a Hayes-compatible modem (AT command set) at 1200 or
2400 baud minimum; 9600 or 14400 if you can swing it. A second
phone line means a second modem, second copy of the BBS software,
and a router or rotary on the upstream side -- this is no small
thing.

[--[ SOFTWARE ]--]

PCBoard. WildCat!. Maximus. Renegade. Spitfire. Each has
opinions about menus, file handling, and what colors a sysop should
have access to. Pick one, learn it deeply, and do not change it
unless you must -- migrating message bases between programs is the
fastest way to end up with three months of conversation in a flat
file you cannot read.

[--[ FILE AREAS ]--]

Typical layout, by section:
    A) Utilities
    B) Games (the largest section, regardless of your intent)
    C) Programming and source
    D) Communications
    E) Text files (the section that will outlive the rest)
    F) New uploads (curate weekly into the lettered sections)

[--[ RATIO ]--]

Files cost upload bytes. The going ratio is 1:5 -- one byte uploaded
buys five bytes of download. Some boards run 1:10 or none-at-all
("leech" boards, generally short-lived). Whatever you set, enforce
it before the user, not after, and post the policy on the main menu.

Set a daily-time limit (60 minutes is generous, 30 is normal,
15 is "we have one line and many users").

[--[ MESSAGE BASES ]--]

A message base is a forum: subject lines, threading, and a daily
quota of new posts. Local-only bases stay on your board. Echomail
bases travel via FidoNet (see fidonet-overview.txt). You will spend
more sysop hours moderating message bases than running the
hardware. Plan accordingly.

[--[ THE PEOPLE ]--]

Most users are quiet and you will never hear from them. A small
fraction will write you long messages about what is wrong with your
file ratios. A smaller fraction will try to break into the door
games to inflate their stats; this is not interesting and not worth
chasing. The interesting people show up in the message bases.

[--[ FOR THE LOVE OF THE SYSOP ]--]

Run weekly backups to floppies (preferably high-density, preferably
a different brand than your daily floppies, preferably stored at a
friend's house). Keep your modem firmware where you remember it.
Document your DEFAULTS.CFG. When you go down for maintenance, post
about it ahead of time, and come back when you said you would.

This is the entire handbook. The rest is detail.
